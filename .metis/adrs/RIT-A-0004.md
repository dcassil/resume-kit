# ADR-4: Guidance-to-capability classification and shared severity taxonomy for resume guidance

Supports [[RIT-I-0018]] — the industry guidance audit. **Decided 2026-08-05 by Daniel Cassil.** It records the two load-bearing framework decisions that govern how the audit turns raw industry guidance into product behavior.

## Context **[REQUIRED]**

RIT-I-0018 takes a large body of industry resume "do's and don'ts" (compiled from major ATS vendors — Workable, SmartRecruiters, Jobvite, Lever, Indeed, LinkedIn) and must decide, **per guidance item**, how the product should express it. The initiative is explicitly anti-"add every checklist item as a hard rule": some guidance is a deterministic check, some is an LLM/reviewer prompt, some is skill/workflow copy or documentation, some remains human judgment, and some requires genuinely new ingest/export inspection that does not yet exist.

Two framework decisions must be fixed **before** the audit produces its decision matrix, because every row depends on them and because sibling initiatives are already building the surfaces the audit will target:

1. **How is each guidance item classified into a product expression?** Without a fixed classification scheme, the audit produces an inconsistent matrix and risks converting soft advice into noisy hard gates (the exact anti-goal).
2. **What severity/output vocabulary do findings use?** [[RIT-I-0016]] ([[RIT-A-0003]]) is concurrently establishing a grooming-finding severity taxonomy for its best-practices engine. If RIT-I-0018 invents a second taxonomy, the product ends up with two incompatible ways of ranking resume findings, and the two initiatives' outputs cannot be merged or surfaced together.

There are also hard constraints inherited from decided/September-adjacent work that the classification must respect:
- **[[RIT-A-0002]] / [[RIT-I-0017]]** make **ScoreDoc** the single deterministic, offline, no-LLM read model for all scoring, and own the one "what the ATS sees" report. Any guidance item that lands in the scoring path must read ScoreDoc and stay deterministic; anything inherently judgment-based must NOT enter that path.
- **[[RIT-I-0016]] / [[RIT-A-0003]]** own the `original → base → standard` lineage, the job-INDEPENDENT best-practices engine and dos/donts slice, and **REQ-011** (bounded source-file PDF/DOCX layout parse-risk detection).
- Truth/faithfulness gates (`resume_validate_truth`, `candidate_evidence_build`, `resume_validate_faithfulness`) and the [[RIT-I-0015]] edit-session orchestrator are shared guardrails, reused not rebuilt.

This ADR fixes decisions (1) and (2) so the audit is consistent and non-duplicative.

## Decision **[REQUIRED]**

**Decision A — a fixed six-lane guidance-to-capability classification.** Every atomized guidance item in the audit's decision matrix is assigned exactly one **primary product-expression lane** (it may carry secondary lanes for surfacing, but exactly one primary determines where the behavior is built):

1. **Deterministic check** — computable offline from existing structured data (`ResumeDocument`/BuildDoc, `JobDescription`, `CandidateEvidence`) **via ScoreDoc** when scoring-adjacent. Must obey RIT-A-0002's determinism/offline/no-LLM invariant.
2. **LLM / reviewer prompt** — judgment-based (relevance framing, coherent voice, natural-sounding wording). Routed to the review/second-agent surfaces (`review-resume`) or the `standard` walkthrough — **never** into the deterministic scoring path.
3. **Skill / workflow copy** — guidance best expressed as instruction or prompting inside a skill or the `resume-workflow` guide (e.g. "review parsed fields after upload").
4. **Documentation** — belongs in README/docs, not enforced by tooling.
5. **Human judgment (no tooling)** — recorded as guidance the user owns; the product does not act on it.
6. **Out-of-scope / future capability** — requires new ingest/export inspection that does not exist yet (layout/render parse checks, PDF text-layer/selectable-text verification). **These defer to their owning capability** — layout/parse-risk to [[RIT-I-0016]] **REQ-011**, export-side selectable-text to a future `export`/ingest capability — and are NOT re-scoped by RIT-I-0018.

**Decision B — adopt RIT-I-0016's severity taxonomy as the single shared vocabulary.** Findings across the whole system use one taxonomy, owned and defined by [[RIT-I-0016]] / [[RIT-A-0003]]:

- **hard-gate** — blocks a write/commit (reserved for truth/faithfulness-class failures; guidance items rarely qualify).
- **warning** — surfaced prominently; should be addressed.
- **recommendation** — suggested improvement.
- **review-note** — advisory context for a human reviewer.
- **out-of-scope-future** — acknowledged but not yet actionable (maps to lane 6).

RIT-I-0018 **adopts and may propose extensions** to this taxonomy (extensions flow back to RIT-A-0003 as the owner); it does **not** define a parallel severity model. Mapping rule: lane 1 items are typically warning/recommendation, lane 2 items are review-note, lane 6 items are out-of-scope-future. **Hard-gate is reserved** and requires explicit justification per item so generic advice is never silently promoted to a blocker (the initiative's core anti-goal).

**Ownership corollary.** RIT-I-0018 owns the **audit + decision matrix + the single consolidated guidance inventory** (job-dependent remainder plus a reference to RIT-I-0016's job-independent slice). It builds no new scoring substrate, no second report, and no competing severity model; where a lane-1 item needs a new deterministic check, it is implemented on the ScoreDoc-based engines the siblings own.

## Alternatives Analysis **[CONDITIONAL: Complex Decision]**

| Option | Pros | Cons | Risk Level | Implementation Cost |
|--------|------|------|------------|-------------------|
| **A. Fixed six-lane classification + adopt RIT-I-0016 taxonomy (CHOSEN)** | One consistent matrix; findings merge across initiatives; respects ScoreDoc/determinism + REQ-011 ownership; hard-gate reserved so advice ≠ blocker | Requires up-front agreement with RIT-I-0016 on the taxonomy; some items need judgment calls on primary lane | Low | Low (framework only; discovery artifact) |
| **B. No fixed scheme — classify ad hoc per item** | Fastest to start | Inconsistent matrix; likely two severity models; high risk of promoting advice to noisy hard gates | High | Low up front, high rework |
| **C. Define RIT-I-0018's own severity taxonomy** | Self-contained | Two incompatible finding vocabularies; outputs can't be surfaced together; contradicts RIT-A-0003 ownership | Medium–High | Medium |
| **D. Treat everything as either "check" or "doc" (two lanes)** | Simple | Collapses judgment-based and future-capability items into wrong homes; forces LLM-shaped guidance into deterministic checks (violates RIT-A-0002) | Medium | Low |

## Rationale **[REQUIRED]**

The audit's value is a **consistent, non-duplicative** decision matrix. A fixed lane set (Option A) is what makes rows comparable and prevents the two failure modes the initiative explicitly warns against: promoting soft advice to hard gates, and overclaiming that layout/parse guidance is "covered" when it needs source-file inspection (lane 6 makes that explicit and hands it to the right owner). Adopting RIT-I-0016's taxonomy rather than minting a new one (rejecting Option C) is the only choice that lets 0016's best-practices findings and 0018's guidance findings coexist in one surface. Options B and D either produce inconsistency or force items into wrong lanes, and D directly violates the ScoreDoc determinism invariant. The framework is cheap — it is a discovery-phase decision, not code — but it is load-bearing for every downstream task, which is exactly what an ADR is for.

## Consequences **[REQUIRED]**

### Positive
- One consistent guidance decision matrix; rows are comparable and auditable with an evidence/source column.
- Findings from RIT-I-0016 (best-practices) and RIT-I-0018 (guidance) share one severity vocabulary and can be surfaced together.
- Determinism/offline/no-LLM scoring invariant (RIT-A-0002) is structurally protected: judgment items are routed to lane 2, never the scoring path.
- Layout/selectable-text guidance is explicitly parked in lane 6 with a named owner (REQ-011 / future export capability), preventing overclaim and duplicate scoping.
- Hard-gate is reserved, so generic advice cannot silently become a blocker.

### Negative
- Requires an up-front coordination handshake with RIT-I-0016 on the taxonomy (a cross-initiative dependency, however small).
- Some guidance items have a genuinely ambiguous primary lane and will need a documented judgment call during the audit.

### Neutral
- This ADR fixes the *framework*; the per-item classifications themselves are produced by the audit and reviewed by a human before RIT-I-0018 exits discovery (they are not decided here).
- The six lanes intentionally mirror the earlier "applies here vs. applies elsewhere" split used when scoping RIT-I-0016, keeping the two initiatives' mental models aligned.

## Review Schedule **[CONDITIONAL: Temporary Decision]**

### Review Triggers
- RIT-I-0016 / RIT-A-0003 changes the severity taxonomy after this ADR is decided (this ADR's Decision B must be re-synced).
- The audit surfaces a guidance item that fits none of the six lanes cleanly (the lane set may need a seventh, or a definition tightened).
- A new ingest/export inspection capability lands that promotes a batch of lane-6 items to lane 1/3 (revisit those rows).

### Scheduled Review
- **Next Review Date**: When RIT-I-0018 transitions discovery → design (the per-item matrix is reviewed then; confirm the framework still holds).
- **Review Criteria**: Whether the six lanes + shared taxonomy still cleanly classify every atomized guidance item without forcing wrong-lane placements or a second severity model.
- **Sunset Date**: N/A — intended as a permanent framework decision for the audit and any future guidance ingestion.
